# **Why Product Roles Are Changing Right Now**

*AI has fundamentally changed how products are built, pushing PMs to redefine their roles.*

Product development has been irreversibly transformed by [AI-coding assistance](https://priankr.substack.com/p/building-with-ai). These advancements are great for shipping products faster, but they force product organizations to reassess *how much* their product managers contribute to development. [Some](https://www.businessinsider.com/linkedin-product-manager-apm-full-stack-builder-2025-12) have even gone as far as to get rid of the role entirely. While AI does not *eliminate* the need for product work, it does make it possible to *detach* many product tasks from the PM role, weakening the role’s traditional value proposition. Sometimes these are reassigned to other team members equipped with AI tools, and sometimes to AI agents.

The tech industry has realized that AI makes it possible for product teams operate with fewer people. Companies seem determined to get “leaner” while still achieving similar or greater levels of performance, productivity, and profit. [Many](https://www.sfgate.com/tech/article/october-2025-tech-industry-layoffs-blood-bath-21143585.php) are many actively laying off (or planning to lay off) people as they move to downsize teams. PMs are especially vulnerable during this time because they typically manage execution, but don’t directly build the product themselves. Companies are increasingly interested in “full-stack” professionals, who can work across product, design, and engineering.

AI has already proven to be decently capable of automating the time-consuming tasks involved in coordinating product development (e.g., creating documentation, tracking progress, communicating updates). As AI tools get more capable, companies will increasingly expect PMs to possess broader skillsets. Therefore, PMs can no longer restrict themselves to merely *managing* the work. They need to *do* the work too. The possibility of [role consolidation](https://www.aha.io/blog/the-new-product-manager-in-the-era-of-role-consolidation) forces *all* product team members to reassess how they add value to product development. PMs who spend most of their time [facilitating](https://www.svpg.com/the-era-of-the-product-creator/) the work are in real trouble.

> I have been emphasizing that the heart of the product manager’s job is *product creation*. The job is *not* about being a facilitator or cheerleader, it’s not about being a project manager, and it’s definitely not about being a backlog administrator.
> 
> 
> — [*Marty Cagan, Silicon Valley Product Group*](https://www.svpg.com/the-era-of-the-product-creator/)
